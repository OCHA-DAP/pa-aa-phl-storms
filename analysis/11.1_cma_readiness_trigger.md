# Readiness Trigger — CMA Operational Forecast Analysis (2026 Revision)

**Notebook:** `11.1_cma_readiness_trigger.ipynb`

Tests the readiness trigger threshold against historical CMA (China Meteorological Administration) Western North Pacific tropical cyclone bulletin data. Unlike the IBTrACS analysis (notebook 11), which verifies thresholds against observed best-track winds, this notebook asks what the **operational CMA forecast** showed 4–7 days before qualifying landfall.

## Data and Methodology

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Source | CMA BABJ bulletin files (`ds-cma-datasharing/cma_ftp/data_out/2004-2025_WNP_TC/`) |
| Format | BABJ `.dat` files — one per storm, 6-hourly bulletins, four rows per bulletin: FH=0 (observed analysis), FH=24/48/72 (forecast positions) |
| Record | 2004–2025 (22 years), 531 storms assessed |
| Wind speed | CMA 2-minute sustained winds. Converted: m/s × 1.944 → knots |
| Landfall detection | FH=0 (observed analysis) consecutive positions connected into LineString segments; storm counted only if a segment crosses the actual land boundary of a target region (no buffer) |
| Trigger logic | **Readiness activates if:** (1) the storm made a qualifying landfall at ≥ threshold, AND (2) any CMA bulletin issued **4–7 days before** that landfall showed ≥ threshold in any forecast row (FH=0/24/48/72) |
| Activation rule | Once per season — if multiple storms qualify in the same year, it counts as one activation |

**Why forecast rows, not just observed track:**
> The CMA BABJ bulletin contains the operational forecast issued at each 6-hour step. FH=0 is the analysis (what the storm was doing at that time). FH=24/48/72 are the CMA forecasts for the following 1–3 days. Readiness should reflect what was operationally foreseeable at the time a forecast was issued, not just what the storm was observed to be doing 4–7 days before landfall.

**Wind speed conversion:**
> 177 kph (1-min) × 0.93 = **164.6 kph (2-min)** = **88.9 kt** — compared directly against CMA 2-minute wind values.
> 192 kph (1-min) × 0.93 = **178.6 kph (2-min)** = **96.4 kt** — test threshold.

## Lead Time Analysis

For each storm with a qualifying landfall, the earliest CMA bulletin that showed ≥ threshold in any FH row gives the maximum available lead time.

| Minimum lead time required | Seasons | Return period (yrs) |
|---------------------------|---------|---------------------|
| ≥ 4 days | 7 | 3.1 |
| ≥ 5 days | 3 | 7.3 |
| ≥ 6 days | 3 | 7.3 |
| ≥ 7 days | 1 | 22.0 |

The 4–7 day readiness window captures all 7 activated seasons. Tightening the minimum lead time to 5 days loses 4 seasons — storms where the CMA forecast only reached threshold within 4–5 days of landfall. Only one season (2018: Mangkhut or Yutu) had a qualifying forecast signal at 7+ days lead.

## Design Considerations

- **Region VIII (Eastern Visayas) has zero CMA activations.** This is the most significant difference from the IBTrACS analysis, where Region VIII had 5 activated seasons (including Haiyan 2013 and Rai 2021). Those storms made catastrophic landfalls in Region VIII but the CMA forecast 4–7 days before did not show them at ≥ threshold intensity — they intensified rapidly in the final days. This is a critical limitation of the forecast-based trigger: very rapid intensifiers close to landfall are systematically missed.

- **Region II (Cagayan Valley) dominates.** 6 of 7 activated seasons involve Region II. Its northeast-facing Pacific coast intercepts westward-tracking typhoons at peak intensity, making CMA forecasts reliable well in advance.

- **177 kph vs 192 kph makes little difference overall (3.1 vs 3.7 years).** Both thresholds capture the same core set of storms. The main loss at 192 kph is Kammuri (2019) in Region V.

- **The 4-day minimum lead is operationally binding.** All 7 activated seasons had a qualifying CMA forecast within the 4–7 day window. Reducing the minimum to 3 days would likely add some storms; extending the minimum to 5+ days would drop 4 seasons. The 4-day floor is appropriate given NDRRMC/OCD pre-positioning timelines.

- **Comparison with IBTrACS (notebook 11):** IBTrACS gives 15 activated seasons over 2000–2024 at the equivalent threshold. CMA gives 7 over 2004–2025. The gap reflects both the shorter record and the forecast constraint — IBTrACS counts any storm that *made landfall* at threshold, while CMA requires the forecast to have *predicted* it at threshold 4–7 days out. The CMA result is the operationally relevant one for trigger design.
