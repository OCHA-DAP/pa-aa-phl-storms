# 13.1 — Landfall Rainfall: IMERG Observations & CHIRPS-GEFS Forecasts

Evaluates a rainfall-based observational trigger using IMERG daily data and
assesses how closely CHIRPS-GEFS rainfall forecasts can reproduce IMERG
observations at different lead times before landfall.

---

## Setup

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Data source (observed) | IMERG daily (mean rainfall per region) |
| Data source (forecast) | CHIRPS-GEFS daily (admin1 zonal mean, 0.05°) |
| IMERG record | 1998–2024 (27 years) |
| CHIRPS-GEFS record | 2001–2024 |
| Regions | PH02 Cagayan Valley, PH03 Central Luzon, PH05 Bicol, PH08 Eastern Visayas |
| Landfall source | IBTrACS WP best-track |
| Min wind speed | 34 kt (tropical storm threshold) |
| Rainfall window | Day −1, landfall day, Day +1 (3-day total) |
| Metric | Max 3-day accumulated mean rainfall (mm) across the four regions |
| GEFS lead times | 1–5 days before landfall (lead 0 not available: window includes day −1) |

**Landfall detection**: a storm is counted when a consecutive 6-hourly track
segment crosses into the target-region boundary. First crossing only.

**CHIRPS-GEFS matching**: for each storm and lead time, the forecast issued
`lead` days before landfall is used to accumulate the same 3-day window.
Skill comparisons are restricted to the common 2001–2024 period (117 storms).

## IMERG latency context

IMERG Late Run has ~18-hour latency per day. To accumulate the full 3-day
window (day −1, day 0, day +1 around landfall), IMERG data would not be
complete until roughly 2 days after landfall — making it unusable for
anticipatory action. CHIRPS-GEFS fills this gap.

| Lead | Issued | Use case |
|---|---|---|
| Lead 1 | Day before landfall | Best skill; still allows ~24 h for activation |
| Lead 3 | 3 days before landfall | Operationally recommended; R = 0.44–0.69 |
| Lead 5 | 5 days before landfall | Lowest skill; R = 0.40–0.53 |

Lead 3 is recommended as the operational sweet-spot: enough lead time for
anticipatory action while maintaining moderate correlation with IMERG.

---

## Key observations

- **Lead 1 consistently gives the highest skill** across all regions — CHIRPS-GEFS
  issued one day before landfall correlates most strongly with IMERG (R = 0.58–0.86).
- **Region VIII (Eastern Visayas) shows the strongest skill at lead 1** (R = 0.857,
  R² = 0.73), likely because its narrower landfall exposure area is better
  resolved by both products.
- **GEFS is systematically dry at longer leads** — bias turns increasingly
  negative beyond lead 1, reaching −44 to −48 mm at lead 5. This means
  GEFS underestimates rainfall further from landfall.
- **Lead 1 bias is small** (−8 to +12 mm), suggesting GEFS captures the
  magnitude well when issued close to the event.
- **Rank overlap**: CHIRPS-GEFS at lead 3 recovers most of the IMERG top-5
  storms per region, confirming that the relative severity ordering is
  largely preserved even at medium range.
- High-rainfall storms are **not confined to the strongest typhoons** — Aere
  (65 km/h), Ketsana (74 km/h), and Nock-ten (65 km/h) all produced > 300 mm
  at landfall despite weak winds, driven by moisture and slow movement.
